The approach

01

I come at growth from two directions most people never combine.

A quarter-century of painting and a decade inside the art world taught me to judge whether a thing is any good — the taste half. Performance marketing taught me to prove it with money and data — the rigor half. Because I build the automation layer myself, the two stop being a handoff between departments: a creative call becomes a test, a test becomes a workflow, and the workflow runs without me.

That last part is where most of my time goes now — turning the parts of marketing that used to need a person into agents that don't.

cat. 02

Reading an account against its own assumptions

Paid acquisition & creative analysis — European telehealth (client anonymized)

Outcome3 targeting beliefs overturned by the data

A go-to-market and creative audit for a telehealth brand entering a new market. The interesting part wasn't the plan — it was where the campaign data flatly contradicted what everyone assumed was working:

  • Static creative beat UGC on share of genuinely new customers.
  • Broad targeting beat lookalikes — the "safe" audience was the expensive one.
  • Hook rate barely correlated with acquisition — the vanity metric wasn't the buying signal.

The deliverable: a UGC scoring rubric and a media read that told the client which of their beliefs the numbers didn't support.

› Method & framing note
Presented as approach and analytical calls only — the client's brief, names, and raw data are withheld. The value on display is the diagnostic method: isolating new-customer share from re-engagement, separating creative-attention metrics from purchase behaviour, and pressure-testing targeting orthodoxy against spend.

The path here

04

I started as a painter, and I've never stopped.

Twenty-five years at the easel, an MA in contemporary art from Sotheby's Institute in New York, and a run directing a contemporary gallery in Tel Aviv taught me the part of this work you can't A/B test: whether something is actually any good. Alongside it, a computer science degree from IDC Herzliya gave me the other half — I could always build the thing, not just brief it.

Those two tracks stayed separate for years until performance marketing turned out to be where they finally met: a discipline that rewards taste and demands proof, and that increasingly runs on the exact automation I like to build. So that's what I do now — sit at the seam between the marketer, the builder, and the artist, and make the machine in the middle run itself.
