## Hook

How do you launch a weight-loss brand in a country that bans advertising the medication?

## Insight

In 2025 the UK cracked down on advertising prescription weight-loss medication to the public: you can't name the drug, show the injection pen, say "injection" or "GLP-1," or run influencer discount codes. Most launch playbooks break the moment they hit that rule.

So instead of fighting the constraint, I made it the strategy. I built a fictional clinician-led brand, **Begin**, that advertises the *consultation and the outcome* — never the medicine. Then I worked it end to end like a real launch: positioning in the gap between the cheap end and the premium clinics, buyer personas, a test-and-scale media plan, creative direction, and the one metric that actually matters. The thinking is packaged as an 11-card carousel built to be understood while thumb-scrolling — no narration needed.

## How it works

1. **Map** the constraint — what the rules forbid, and what's still allowed.
2. **Position** in the gap — clinical credibility, minus the premium price and the shame.
3. **Plan** the media — test cheap on paid search and social, scale only the proven winner.
4. **Direct** the creative — sell the feeling or the trust; never show the drug.
5. **Measure** what counts — new customers, not the cheapest-looking conversion.

## What I learned

The sharpest lesson was a data one: the lowest cost-per-conversion often hides returning customers, and the ad with the best "hook" rarely drives the best *acquisition*. Optimising for the flattering, visible metric quietly works against the real goal. The advertising constraint forced more honest measurement — which turned out to be the real takeaway: in a regulated category, the limitation isn't a problem to route around, it's the positioning.
