The approach

01

I came to AI as a marketer, not an engineer.

Running paid acquisition across Google, Meta and Bing, in the US, UK and 12 European markets, showed me where the time actually goes. It isn't strategy. It's the manual middle: pulling reports, fixing tracking, remaking creative versions, replying to leads, setting up the next test. Ideas waited in a queue for a developer to build the tooling, or for a designer to prepare the creatives.

So I build it: a Python tool that turns a competitor's ad into a brief and storyboard, a quiz funnel with three A/B variants, a voice agent that answers visitors' questions, and n8n workflows that route leads on their own. The metrics stay the same — CPA, CAC, ROAS, conversion rate. What gets shorter is the loop from idea to test to decision. I treat each automation as a hypothesis until the numbers back it.

cat. 02

Reading an account against its own assumptions

Paid acquisition & creative analysis — European telehealth (brand anonymized)

Outcome3 targeting beliefs overturned by the data

A go-to-market and creative audit for a telehealth brand entering a new market. The interesting part wasn't the plan — it was where the campaign data flatly contradicted what everyone assumed was working:

  • Static creative beat UGC on share of genuinely new customers.
  • Broad targeting beat lookalikes — the "safe" audience was the expensive one.
  • Hook rate barely correlated with acquisition — the vanity metric wasn't the buying signal.

The deliverable: a UGC scoring rubric and a media read showing which of the brand's beliefs the numbers didn't support.

› Method & framing note
Presented as approach and analytical calls only — the brand's name, brief and raw data are withheld. The value on display is the diagnostic method: isolating new-customer share from re-engagement, separating creative-attention metrics from purchase behaviour, and pressure-testing targeting orthodoxy against spend.
